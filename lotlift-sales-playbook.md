# LotLift sales playbook

## Call objective

Earn a **15-minute Lead Response Workflow Check**. It does not sell LotLift on the first call.

## North star

The cold call earns a **15-minute Lead Response Workflow Check**. It does not sell LotLift on the first call.

Use this order: reach the right owner → establish relevant problem context → learn one fact about their workflow → book the check.

LotLift is a lead-coverage workflow for **approved inbound online inquiry sources**. It helps independent dealerships see and consistently handle leads when the team is busy or after hours.

## Policy tactic metadata

- **permission-and-route:** Ask for the workflow owner or permission for one discovery turn.
- **workflow-discovery:** Learn one verified source, arrival, ownership, after-hours, or visibility fact.
- **impact-clarification / solution-verification:** Clarify consequence or test stated existing coverage without assuming a gap.
- **decision-criteria / concern-isolation:** Surface one blocker before answering price, value, partner, or alternative concerns.
- **consented-follow-up / scoped-next-step:** Require prospect consent; a workflow check also requires source, owner, gap, and authority evidence.
- **truthful-limitation / respectful-exit:** State supported boundaries, honor opt-outs, and end cleanly when disqualified.

Policy rationale: earn each conversation advance with verified prospect evidence, ask one answerable question when a fact is missing, and use only approved product or policy facts. Never override an opt-out or fill an evidence gap with a claim.

## Product facts

**Statement:** LotLift helps teams create a workflow for approved inbound lead sources.
**Category:** capability

### product:lead-ownership
**Statement:** Basically, we make sure the lead gets a response, somebody owns it, and the customer can move toward an appointment even when your team is busy or the store is closed.
**Category:** capability

## Ideal customer and fit

Prioritize independent used-car dealers with meaningful used inventory, visible marketplace listings, a small sales team, and an identifiable owner, GM, sales manager, internet manager, or BDC manager.

**Disqualify or pause** when the dealer has no meaningful supported inbound source, lead volume is too low, direct CRM/DMS integration is a hard requirement, the provider does not authorize the feed, required inbox access is not permitted, security/data requirements cannot be met, or their existing process genuinely covers the problem.

Never claim LotLift supports every marketplace, replaces a CRM/DMS/BDC, guarantees ROI, or can access customer data or internal systems without explicit authorization.

## Before each call

Record only: dealership, contact/role, a verified public signal, likely problem, one tailored context line, and one discovery question. Research for 2–4 minutes, not 20.

Example context: “I was looking at your Cars.com inventory…”

Example question: “Who owns an inquiry after business hours?”

## Approved opening

> “Hey [First Name]—it’s Isaiah, founder of LotLift. I was looking at [Dealership]’s used inventory on [Cars.com / CarGurus / AutoTrader]. I’ll be straight with you, this is a cold call, but it’s a specific one. Can I take 30 seconds to tell you why I called?”

Stop and wait. Do not continue without a response.

## Problem framing

> “The pattern I’m trying to understand is this: a shopper sends a paid online inquiry late in the day, it lands in the shared inbox or gets handed around, everybody assumes somebody else has it, and by the next morning that shopper has messaged a few other stores. I’m not saying that happens at [Dealership]—I do not know your process yet. LotLift is built to help independent dealers create a coverage workflow around approved inbound lead sources, so the team can see who owns the lead and move it into a consistent response and appointment process. Is that a process you feel really confident in today?”

Do not dump features, pretend certainty about their workflow, or ask for a meeting before relevance exists.

## Discovery sequence

Ask one useful question, listen, then follow the thread. Do not interrogate.

1. **Lead sources:** Which online sources generate meaningful buyer inquiries? Which are paid?
2. **Current workflow:** Where does a new inquiry arrive first? Who owns it? What happens after hours, when someone is busy, off, or forgets?
3. **Visibility:** How do they know every lead got a real response? When was one missed, delayed, or unclear?
4. **Pain:** What does that create for the team? Where does the process break: speed, follow-up, booking, no-shows, or something else?
5. **Quantify:** “If one extra paid inquiry turned into a real appointment and sale, what would that be worth?” Capture their metric, value, and exact evidence; never invent a number.
6. **Current solution:** What CRM/lead process exists? Does the inquiry land directly and get assigned, or is there an inbox/handoff? Is there a BDC? Do supported sources reach Gmail/Google Workspace?
7. **Authority and urgency:** Who else must be comfortable? Is direct CRM/DMS integration required? What changes or dates make the issue urgent?

Useful follow-ups:

- Shared inbox: “If it comes in after hours or the usual person is off, how is ownership decided?”
- CRM: “Does it get assigned immediately, or is there still an inbox or handoff?”
- BDC: “How do you verify coverage when the team is overloaded or closed?”
- Salespeople: “How do you know two people are not assuming the other person got it?”
- Strong process: “What specifically gives you confidence—reporting, CRM assignment, a BDC, or active inbox monitoring?”

## Qualification rules

A scoped evaluation needs all of these: one dealership, authorized/supported lead sources, a named workflow owner, an agreed behavior scope, measurable success criteria, a review cadence, and a fixed paid-conversion decision date.

A promising call has a real workflow gap, meaningful enough volume/impact, a truthfully supported scope, an authority path, and a time-bound next step.

Do not offer open-ended free access. Do not force a sale where the workflow is already covered or where LotLift cannot operate safely.

## Summarize before showing anything

> “Let me make sure I have this right. Your [source] leads currently arrive in [place]. [Person/team] is supposed to own them. The gap or risk is [their words], especially when [specific situation]. The result is [their consequence]. Is that accurate?”

Do not demo until they confirm. Show only the workflow that answers their expressed concern.

## Close rules

Once they admit uncertainty, a manual gap, inconsistency, or genuine curiosity:

> “Rather than try to pitch you cold, I think the useful next step would be a short lead-response workflow check. We would look at which sources matter, where the leads land, who owns them after hours, and whether LotLift could realistically help. If it is not a fit, I’ll tell you that directly. Would Tuesday morning or Thursday afternoon be better for 15 minutes?”

Use two time windows, then confirm a specific time and best email. Ask the key source before ending and send the invite while on the call.

Calendar title: **[Dealership] × LotLift — Lead Response Workflow Check**.

The meeting decides whether LotLift is relevant, not relevant, or worth a scoped evaluation. It is not a product trap.

## Objection response model

Classify first. Pause. Relate. Clarify. Repeat the concern accurately. Reduce risk only with truthful scope, proof, pilot, alternative, or limitation. Ask one specific next step. Exit after two real noes.

### Gatekeeper

Goal: identify the person who owns online lead response; never pitch the receptionist.

- **“What is this regarding?”** — “It’s about how the dealership covers paid online inquiries from marketplace listings. Could you let [Name] know Isaiah from LotLift called?”
- **“Is this a sales call?”** — “It may lead to one if there is a real gap, but right now I’m trying to find out who owns the online-lead response process. Is that [Name], or someone else?”
- **“What does LotLift do?”** — use the one-sentence LotLift definition, then ask who owns the process.
- **“We do not take sales calls.”** — “Understood. I don’t want to break your process. Is there a preferred email for whoever owns paid online inquiries?”
- **“Take us off your list.”** — “Absolutely. I’ll mark this number do-not-call. Thanks for letting me know.” Then stop.

### Reflexes and current solutions

- **“We’re not interested.”** — “Fair enough. Before I close this out, is that because marketplace inquiries are already consistently covered there, or because it isn’t a priority right now?”
- **“We already have a CRM.”** — “That makes sense. When a marketplace inquiry comes in, does it go straight into that workflow and get picked up right away, or is there still an inbox or handoff first?” If it’s truly covered: “LotLift probably isn’t useful there.”
- **“We have a BDC / internet department.”** — “That is usually the right setup. How does the team verify coverage after hours or when the usual person is off?”
- **“Our salespeople handle it.”** — “How is ownership decided when two people are busy or it comes in late?”
- **“We’re happy with what we have.”** — “Good—if it is working, you should keep it. What are you using to make sure paid online inquiries do not sit unworked?”
- **“We use a competitor.”** — “What made you choose them, and what does that process handle well?” Then ask whether routing, after-hours response, or appointments remain manual.
- **“We tried something like this.”** — “What specifically broke down—the lead source, setup, responses, team use, or results?”

### Fit, timing, and information requests

- **Unsupported source:** “Then I may have the wrong store. Which sources generate most buyer inquiries?” Disqualify if unsupported/not meaningful.
- **Low lead volume:** “That may mean LotLift is not worth adding. Roughly how many paid online inquiries do you get in a normal month?”
- **Too small:** “Smaller teams are either a great fit because coverage is hard, or not a fit because volume is too low. How many people share online lead response?”
- **Busy:** “I do not want to pitch you in the middle of it. Is there a calmer 15-minute window this week, or should I close this out?”
- **Call later:** “What is changing later that would make this a better conversation then?” Record a real event/date; otherwise offer two concrete times or close out.
- **Send information:** “So I do not send generic software material, what are you most trying to understand: current-process fit, supported sources, security, or pricing?” Send only that, then seek a real follow-up.
- **Pricing before discovery:** Ask two questions about source and after-hours ownership. If they refuse, give only the published truthful range; never invent a quote.
- **Contract:** “When does that agreement renew, and does it already cover the inbox and after-hours handoff?” Record the actual renewal date.

### Product, security, and authorization

- **CRM/DMS integration:** “Today, LotLift is not a CRM or DMS integration. It works around approved inbox lead flows and appointment workflow. Is direct CRM/DMS integration a hard requirement?” Cleanly disqualify if yes.
- **Every marketplace:** “No. We only support sources and workflows that are authorized and validated for the dealership. Which sources matter most?”
- **AI / automated communication:** “The important question is what action you would authorize and how it would be reviewed. We do not turn on behavior outside the approved workflow.”
- **Data access:** “That is a sensible concern. We should determine whether data processing, permissions, retention, and security materials meet your requirements. If they do not, we should not proceed.”
- **Provider forbids feed:** “Then we will not use that source.”
- **Missed lead/wrong response:** “We would define supported sources, test the workflow, set ownership, and agree the fallback process. If we cannot do that safely, we should not enable it.”

### Buying and close objections

- **Too expensive / no budget:** “I hear you. Is the concern the monthly spend itself, the setup effort, another option, or whether closing the gap feels worth it?” Isolate the real concern before discussing any price, scope, or offer.
- **Need to think:** “What is the biggest thing you want to feel certain about before deciding?” Then ask whether anything else prevents a yes.
- **Need owner/partner:** “What will they want to know before they’re comfortable?” Invite them to a focused decision call.
- **Comparing competitors:** “What criteria are you using? Where do you see LotLift as stronger or weaker?” Do not conceal limitations.
- **Free trial:** “What would need to be true after 30 days for you to decide it is worth paying for?” Define controlled scope and end date.
- **ROI guarantee:** “I cannot responsibly guarantee a number before we know your lead volume, current process, team follow-up, source mix, and approved workflow.” Agree measurable operating outcomes instead.
- **Company is too new:** “That is fair. We are early, and I will not pretend otherwise.” Offer only a limited, controlled scope; otherwise revisit later.

## What not to say

Never say or imply: “Mike told me to call,” “it’s personal,” “I’m returning his call,” “this is not a sales call,” untrue local dealership proof, unsupported integrations, blanket automation promises, guaranteed ROI, invented pricing, or that their process mishandles leads.

Never evade opt-out requests, pressure personal financing, contact a dealer through an unauthorized lead feed, activate a workflow before authorization and controls, argue with a clear no, or respond to an objection with a feature dump before understanding it.

## What the coach must capture

For every meaningful prospect turn, preserve exact evidence and update only supported facts: current solution, source/workflow, quantified pain, authority, urgency/renewal date, recurring objection, prior answer, commitment, open question, buying signal, close opportunity, fit/disqualification reason, and do-not-contact instruction.

A buying signal is curiosity, an acknowledged workflow gap, a request for implementation/security/pricing detail after relevance, agreement to include an authority holder, or acceptance of a scoped next step. A close opportunity requires confirmed pain/gap, viable scope, authority path, and a specific next action.

### objection:not-interested
- **Intent:** Prospect declines interest or priority.
- **When to use:** They say they are not interested, it is not a priority, or no thanks.
- **Objective:** Learn whether coverage is genuinely complete before closing.
- **Approved strategy:** Ask one brief workflow-coverage question, then respect a second no.
- **Good examples:**

- **Prohibited behavior:** Do not argue, feature-dump, or ask more than once after a clear second no.
- **Exit condition:** Coverage is confirmed, or the prospect declines again.

### discovery:after-hours
- **Intent:** Learn coverage outside normal availability.
- **When to use:** The current workflow or owner is known.
- **Objective:** Identify the after-hours fallback process.
- **Approved strategy:** Ask how ownership is decided after hours or when the usual person is off.
- **Good examples:**
  - “If it comes in after hours or the usual person is off, how is ownership decided?”
- **Prohibited behavior:** Do not claim leads are missed without prospect evidence.
- **Exit condition:** The fallback is described, or a gap is acknowledged.

### discovery:visibility
- **Intent:** Learn how the team verifies lead coverage.
- **When to use:** Workflow ownership is being discussed.
- **Objective:** Capture reporting, assignment, or monitoring evidence.
- **Approved strategy:** Ask how they know every lead got a real response.
- **Good examples:**
  - “How do you know every lead got a real response?”
- **Prohibited behavior:** Do not imply the team lacks visibility without evidence.
- **Exit condition:** A verification process or gap is identified.

### qualification:pain
- **Intent:** Identify the business consequence of a workflow gap.
- **When to use:** A source, workflow, or visibility gap has been discussed.
- **Objective:** Capture their stated pain and any metric.
- **Approved strategy:** Ask where the process breaks and what one converted inquiry would be worth.
- **Good examples:**
  - “If one extra paid inquiry turned into a real appointment and sale, what would that be worth?”
- **Prohibited behavior:** Do not invent a value, volume, or ROI number.
- **Exit condition:** Pain is quantified or the prospect cannot support a metric.

### qualification:authority
- **Intent:** Identify stakeholders and approval path.
- **When to use:** A viable workflow gap exists.
- **Objective:** Learn who must be comfortable and what they need.
- **Approved strategy:** Ask who else is involved in a decision.
- **Good examples:**
  - “Who else would need to be comfortable with this workflow?”
- **Prohibited behavior:** Do not assume the current contact can approve.
- **Exit condition:** Stakeholders or the approval path are explicit.

### qualification:urgency
- **Intent:** Learn timing, renewal, or a forcing event.
- **When to use:** Pain and authority are sufficiently clear.
- **Objective:** Capture a real date or reason to act.
- **Approved strategy:** Ask what changes or dates make the issue urgent.
- **Good examples:**
  - “What changes or dates make this worth addressing now?”
- **Prohibited behavior:** Do not manufacture urgency.
- **Exit condition:** A date, renewal, event, or lack of urgency is explicit.

### close:workflow-check
- **Intent:** Book a Lead Response Workflow Check.
- **When to use:** There is confirmed pain or uncertainty, viable scope, and an authority path.
- **Objective:** Agree a specific 15-minute next step.
- **Approved strategy:** Offer two time windows and confirm time, source, and email.
- **Good examples:**
  - “Would Tuesday morning or Thursday afternoon be better for 15 minutes?”
- **Prohibited behavior:** Do not demo before confirmed relevance or force an open-ended trial.
- **Exit condition:** A specific meeting is booked, or the prospect declines.
